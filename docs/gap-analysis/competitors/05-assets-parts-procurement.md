## 2. Architecture read

**Atriom's asset-and-parts layer is accounting-grade, not reliability-grade — and that is a coherent, deliberate choice, not an accident.** Everything that touches money is unusually strong: the stock ledger is append-only with derived on-hand (no cached quantity can drift), every movement auto-posts to a real double-entry GL dimensioned to the property, GRNI is a dedicated clearing liability that nets to zero against the vendor bill, the procurement state machine makes "order without approval" *unrepresentable* rather than merely discouraged, and the approval ladder is fail-closed with the tier frozen for audit. Layer on the fixed-asset depreciation twin and the vendor-SLA-penalty-to-bill offset and you have a procure-to-stock-to-books spine that **exceeds what a pure CMMS (Fiix, Facilio) does financially** — those tools lean on an external ERP for exactly this. This is the same derived-truth discipline the GL and AR modules use, applied consistently. Keep it; it is the moat.

**Where it is genuinely thin is the EAM/reliability half — the part Maximo and Fiix exist for.** There is no asset **criticality** ranking, no **condition / downtime / failure-code** capture, and no **meter/usage-based PM** — PM is purely calendar-driven. For a heavy-industrial reliability program that would be disqualifying; for a *mall* it is a real but narrower gap. Malls do run duty-cycle assets where run-hours beat the calendar — escalators, lifts, standby generators, chillers — and Atriom already has a `UtilityMeter`/`MeterReading` primitive it could (but doesn't) repurpose to trigger a plan. The absence of criticality is the more strategic hole: without it, PM priority, SLA tiering, and spare-part stocking levels are all set by feel, and a fire pump sits in the same undifferentiated list as office furniture.

**On the register itself, Atriom matches the specialists on structure and beats them on isolation.** The `Equipment` sub-code tree (acyclic, same-property-guarded, with a compatible-spare-parts pivot and an optional accounting twin) is a well-built asset hierarchy, and the airtight per-property `asset_id` scoping is arguably cleaner for a multi-mall operator than Maximo's org/site model or a bolt-on multi-company workaround. The honest caveats are operational, not structural: standard-costing-only drift (no FIFO/weighted-average), the unfinished warehouse-transfer stub, low-stock that alerts but never drafts a purchase, and no mobile/barcode parts flow for a technician in the field.

**Net architectural read:** Atriom built the *financial* asset-and-parts system a mall operator's accountant and owner want — auditable, GL-integrated, VAT/ETA-correct, per-property — and stopped short of the *reliability-engineering* system a maintenance planner at a Maximo shop would expect. For a mall that is a defensible trade; the gaps below are the ones a Facilio/ServiceChannel customer would feel on day one.

## 3. Top 5 gaps (ranked for a mall operator)

1. **Meter / usage-based PM triggers** *(effort: M)* — escalators, lifts, generators and chillers wear by run-hours and cycles, not the calendar; time-only PM simultaneously over-services light-duty assets and under-services heavy-duty ones. The `MeterReading` primitive exists but feeds billing, not maintenance. Every benchmark does this.
2. **Asset criticality ranking** *(effort: S)* — a single field + surfacing that lets the operator say a fire pump or lift outranks a car-park fan. It is the input that *should* drive PM priority, SLA tiers, and how deep to stock a spare. Cheap to add, disproportionately useful, and today entirely absent.
3. **Condition / downtime / failure-code history → reliability analytics** *(effort: L)* — with no downtime or failure taxonomy, the operator cannot answer "which asset fails most, and what does it cost me?" (MTBF, top offenders, repair-vs-replace). This is the analytics layer Maximo Health and Fiix Foresight are sold on.
4. **Reorder-driven auto-purchase** *(effort: S/M)* — low-stock already fires per-property; turning that signal into a **drafted purchase request** (rather than a bell the storeman must act on manually) closes the loop the benchmarks close automatically, and it reuses the existing procurement flow end-to-end.
5. **Mobile / barcode parts issue, receipt & guided cycle counts** *(effort: M)* — technicians in a Facilio/ServiceChannel shop scan a part off the shelf against a work order and count stock on a phone; Atriom's issue/receive/count are all web forms with no scan and no guided count workflow (counts are ad-hoc adjustment movements). Honourable mentions: weighted-average costing to kill the standard-cost drift, the unfinished warehouse-transfer stub, and multi-quote bid comparison for capex.

## 4. Net verdict

**Behind** the EAM/CMMS specialists on reliability depth (asset criticality, condition/downtime/failure analytics, and meter-based PM); **at par** on the core equipment register, per-property spare-parts inventory, and the procure-to-stock flow; and **ahead** on accounting-grade integration — always-on perpetual-inventory GL costing, GRNI clearing, the fixed-asset depreciation twin, vendor-SLA-penalty-to-bill, airtight per-property isolation, and Egyptian VAT/ETA fit — which the pure CMMS tools push out to an external ERP.
