# Deposits, utilities, tenant portal & owner/portfolio — Atriom vs the mall/retail specialists

> Domain 3 of the competitor gap analysis. Benchmarks Atriom against **Yardi** (Voyager/Commercial + Investment Manager + RentCafe/COMMERCIALCafé), **Re-Leased**, and **AppFolio** — the retail/commercial-PM specialists, not a generic ERP. Atriom side grounded in `DepositService`/`DepositTransaction` + its journalizer, `Payment` (cheque fields), the utility-meter module, the `/portal` + `/owner` Filament panels, the `/api/v1` mobile surface, and `OwnerRequest`. Competitor claims are edition/region/version-sensitive — marked *(verify)*.

## 2. Architecture read

**On deposits, Atriom quietly matches the specialists on the part that's hardest to retrofit — the accounting — and is thin on the part that's easy.** Treating a deposit as a **`Deposits Held` liability** posted through the same journalizer+sweep spine as every other money source (receipt/refund/forfeit, per-lease, per-property, cancellable-with-void, bilingual, race-safe numbering) is the correct, auditable design and is a real head-start over bolting deposits onto a generic GL. What's missing is *workflow*, not *ledger*: there is no per-lease deposit **balance** derived from the event stream, no reconciliation against the lease's contractual `security_deposit` (or the `security_deposit_received` flag), and no single "disposition" that itemises damages and returns the remainder — the operator files a forfeit and a refund as two disconnected events. For a mall that holds 3×-rent deposits on dozens of tenants, "what do we owe back, per tenant, right now" should be one query, and today it isn't.

**The utility design is a deliberate, defensible decoupling that reads as a gap next to Yardi.** Atriom measures usage (meters, readings, delta consumption, cost, trend) and bills money (a `utility` `Charge` on the lease → the monthly run) but **puts a human on the bridge** — no tariff engine multiplies consumption by a rate. The module doc argues this is intentional because Egyptian mall utility re-billing varies per lease (allowance / flat / at-cost / bundled). That's a fair call for correctness, but it means Atriom has no answer to Yardi's convergent/RUBS utility billing where sub-meter reads flow to the invoice automatically, and no smart-meter ingestion. For an operator with hundreds of sub-metered shops, the manual bridge is real monthly labour.

**The tenant self-service story is where Atriom is genuinely at-par with — and in surface breadth arguably ahead of — the mid-market specialists.** A multi-user scoped web portal *plus* a broad versioned mobile API (invoice PDFs, statements, gateway payment initiation, push registration, maintenance create/comment/cancel/rate, sales declarations) is more than Re-Leased typically exposes and comparable to AppFolio's footprint. The one soft spot is the **payment rail**: Paymob is card-only, config-gated, and not certified live, with no ACH/stored-card/autopay — and payments UX is exactly AppFolio's flagship strength, so a prospect comparing on "how does my tenant pay" will feel the difference.

**The owner side is the domain's weakest link and the clearest place a Yardi customer would feel downgraded.** Atriom now has an `/owner` read-only panel and a live portfolio KPI widget — good, and better than the docs claim — plus an owner↔operator ticketing model that most PM tools lack. But it stops at *oversight*. There are **no owner/investor statements, no disbursements/distributions, and no ownership-%-weighted consolidation** — the widget shows a 50% co-owner the whole property's MRR and AR. For an operator like Eltizam running malls *on behalf of* owners like Jawad, the owner **statement + payout** cycle is the core deliverable of the relationship, and it is precisely what Yardi Investment Manager and AppFolio owner accounting are built to produce. This is a completeness gap in the most owner-facing part of the product.

## 3. Top 5 gaps (ranked for a mall operator)

1. **Owner statements + disbursements** *(effort: L)* — no periodic owner/investor statement (income − expenses − management fee = net) and no payout/distribution tracking. For an operator running malls for owners, this *is* the owner deliverable; every benchmark ships it and Atriom ships none of it. Compounded by F-80 (consolidated-only close) and the unused `ownership_percentage`.
2. **Post-dated cheque (PDC) register** *(effort: M)* — the Egyptian norm is a tenant lodging a year of PDCs up front; Atriom captures one cheque number on a *received* payment but has no forward instrument register, maturity schedule, or bounce lifecycle. A market-fit gap the Western tools also miss, so it's a differentiation opportunity, not just parity.
3. **Utility tariff / recharge automation** *(effort: M)* — no reading→charge bridge and no rate engine, so sub-meter re-billing is monthly manual data entry. Yardi's convergent/RUBS billing is the benchmark; even a simple "rate × consumption → utility charge" would close most of the labour.
4. **Deposit balance + reconciliation + itemised refund** *(effort: S)* — the ledger is right but there's no per-lease held-balance view, no reconcile against the contractual deposit, and no single deductions-then-return disposition. Small effort on top of the existing register; high operator value.
5. **Live, richer tenant payment rail** *(effort: M)* — certify Paymob (or add a second gateway), and add stored-card/autopay/ACH-equivalent (InstaPay recurring). Payments UX is AppFolio's headline strength and the most visible thing a tenant touches.

## 4. Net verdict

**At-par to slightly ahead on tenant self-service (portal + mobile API) and on the deposit *ledger*; behind — clearly — on owner statements/disbursements and utility recharge automation, with a market-specific PDC-register gap none of the benchmarks fill either.** The moat is the Egyptian fit (deposit-as-liability in a real bilingual GL, owner↔operator ticketing); the exposure is that the most owner-facing workflow — statements and payouts — is the domain's thinnest.
